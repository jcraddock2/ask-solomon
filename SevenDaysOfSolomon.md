## DAY 1

**Subject line:** The day Solomon changed everything (and what he actually asked for)
**Preview text:** He could have asked for anything. What he chose explains everything.

---

You have probably heard the name Solomon.

Maybe you picture a king on a golden throne. Enormous wealth. A thousand wives. The wisest man who ever lived.

All of that is true. But most people miss the most important part of his story — the part that explains everything else.

Early in his reign, God came to Solomon and said something that has stunned people for three thousand years:

*"Ask what I shall give thee."*

A blank check. From God. Ask for anything.

Solomon could have asked for long life. He could have asked for the destruction of his enemies. He could have asked for wealth beyond measure — and given who was making the offer, he would have received it.

He asked for wisdom.

Specifically, he asked for "an understanding heart" — the ability to discern between good and evil, to lead well, to make decisions that actually worked.

God's response is one of the most remarkable moments in all of scripture:

*"Because you have asked this thing and have not asked for long life or riches or the life of your enemies, I will give you what you asked. And I will also give you what you did not ask — riches and honor, such as no king has had before you or will have after you."*

Solomon did not chase success. He sought wisdom — and success chased him.

That is not a metaphor. It is the thesis of everything you will read in these seven emails.

**The principle:** Wisdom is the principal thing. Seek it first — and everything else follows. Ignore it — and you will spend your life working hard for outcomes that keep slipping away.

Solomon wrote this down in Proverbs so that you would not have to learn it the hard way.

That is why Ask Solomon exists.

## DAY 2

**Subject line:** Your outer life is your inner life, made visible
**Preview text:** Proverbs 4:23 might be the most practical verse in the entire Bible.

---

Yesterday you learned that Solomon asked for wisdom — and received everything else with it.

Today he gives you the most practical instruction in all of Proverbs.

Four words that explain almost every outcome in your life:

*"Keep thy heart with all diligence — for out of it are the issues of life."*
— Proverbs 4:23

"Keep" is a loaded word here. In the original language it means four things simultaneously:
- **Guard** it — protect it from outside attack
- **Inspect** it — search within for what has already taken root
- **Cultivate** it — actively grow what you want inside
- **Maintain** it — tend it consistently, like a garden

And why does this matter so much?

Because *"out of it are the issues of life."* Every outcome in your life — your relationships, your finances, your work, your health, your peace of mind — flows from what is happening inside you.

Solomon said it plainly in Proverbs 23:7: *"As a man thinketh in his heart, so is he."*

Not as he wishes. Not as he intends. As he *thinks.*

Your outer life is your inner life, made visible.

This is not motivational language. It is a law — as reliable as gravity. People who carry bitterness produce bitter outcomes. People who carry fear produce fearful decisions. People who carry faith and clear vision produce lives that reflect both.

The question is not whether your inner world is creating your outer world. It is. The question is whether you are managing what goes in.

**The principle:** You can manage your mind. You must manage your mind. If you do not, other things will — and they do not have your best interests at heart.

Solomon called this "living by design" — deliberately deciding who you are becoming and what fruit you are growing — versus "living by default," where the media, culture, past wounds, and other people's opinions do the planting for you.

## DAY 3

**Subject line:** Death and life are in the power of the tongue — Solomon meant that literally
**Preview text:** The most underestimated force in your daily life.

---

Solomon said something in Proverbs 18:21 that most people read and immediately soften:

*"Death and life are in the power of the tongue."*

We tend to interpret this as a general warning about being kind. Say nice things. Do not gossip. Be careful with your words.

But Solomon meant something far more specific — and far more powerful.

He said your words are creative forces. They do not just describe your life. They help *build* it.

Think about it this way. You speak roughly 16,000 words per day. Of those, a significant portion are about yourself — your abilities, your situation, your future, your worth.

*"I'm terrible with money."*
*"I'm not the kind of person who succeeds at things like that."*
*"This always happens to me."*
*"I'll never be able to change."*

Solomon watched this pattern and said: you are eating the fruit of your words. You are speaking your reality into existence — and then wondering why it keeps showing up.

He gave three specific instructions for the mouth of a wise person:

**1. Think before you speak.** Proverbs 15:28 — "The heart of the righteous *studies* to answer, but the mouth of the wicked pours out evil things." Wise people are not quick with their mouths. They consider the outcome before they produce the output.

**2. Fewer words, more meaning.** Proverbs 17:27 — "He that hath knowledge spareth his words." The wise person talks less and *says* more. Restraint is intelligence.

**3. The soft answer.** Proverbs 15:1 — "A soft answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger." This is what Solomon called the Law of Opposite Behavior — when a situation wants you to escalate, the wisest move is always to go the other direction.

And there is one more principle that cuts the other way:

Proverbs 14:23 — *"In all labor there is profit, but the talk of the lips leads only to poverty."*

Talk without work produces nothing. Words alone — no matter how inspiring, no matter how sincere — do not build anything. They must be paired with action.

**The principle:** Manage your mouth the way you manage your money. Every word is either an investment or a withdrawal.

## DAY 4

**Subject line:** The ant has no manager, no overseer, no ruler — and outworks everyone
**Preview text:** Solomon stopped to study a small insect and found everything we avoid.

---

In the middle of a book about wisdom, wealth, and the meaning of life, Solomon pauses to talk about an ant.

*"Go to the ant, you sluggard. Consider her ways and be wise — which, having no guide, overseer, or ruler, provides her supplies in the summer and gathers her food in the harvest."*
— Proverbs 6:6-8

Most people read this and think it is simply a warning against laziness.

It is much more than that.

Solomon identified three specific qualities in the ant that he considered marks of wisdom:

**1. Industrious.** The ant is productive. Active. Purposeful. It does not wait for inspiration, the right conditions, or external motivation. It simply does what it was made to do — and it does it continuously.

**2. Initiative.** No guide. No overseer. No ruler. The ant is entirely self-directed. It does not need to be told, reminded, or managed. This is what Solomon called the mark of a truly excellent person — someone who does not require external pressure to produce their best work.

**3. Investing.** The ant gathers in summer for winter. It does more than the minimum. It stores what it will need before it needs it. It is future-focused in a way that the sluggard — the person Solomon contrasts with the ant — is not.

The sluggard, by contrast, has *desires.* Proverbs 13:4 says: *"The soul of the sluggard desireth and hath nothing."*

Desire without diligence is just daydreaming.

The difference between the ant and the sluggard is not talent, opportunity, or resources. It is one thing: **the willingness to act without waiting.**

Solomon's conclusion is direct: Proverbs 22:29 — *"Seest thou a man diligent in his business? He shall stand before kings. He shall not stand before obscure men."*

The diligent person will be recognized. Will be promoted. Will be sought out.
Not overnight. Not always on their timeline.
But diligence creates an unstoppable momentum that eventually cannot be ignored.

**The principle:** Activity creates energy. The ant does not wait to feel motivated before it moves — it moves, and the motivation follows. That is the sequence most people have backwards.

## DAY 5

**Subject line:** Solomon's actual wealth blueprint — it is not what most people preach
**Preview text:** He was the wealthiest person in history. Here is what he said about how it works.

---

Solomon was not just wise about money. He was the wealthiest person in recorded history.

Which means when he writes about how wealth works, he is not theorizing. He is reporting from experience.

And his blueprint is surprisingly precise — and in several places, directly counter to conventional wisdom.

**Principle 1: Honor God with the first portion.**
Proverbs 3:9-10 — *"Honor the LORD with your substance and with the firstfruits of all your increase — so shall your barns be filled with plenty and your presses burst with new wine."*

This is the sequence Solomon lived by. Not God gets what is left over. God gets the first portion — and then watch what happens to the rest.

**Principle 2: The diligent hand makes rich.**
Proverbs 10:4 — *"He who has a slack hand becomes poor, but the hand of the diligent makes rich."*

This is cause and effect, not motivation. Consistent, painstaking, self-directed effort over time produces increase. There is no shortcut around this principle.

**Principle 3: Debt is bondage.**
Proverbs 22:7 — *"The rich rules over the poor, and the borrower is servant to the lender."*

Solomon did not say debt is unwise. He said it is a form of servitude. Every payment you owe someone is a measure of your freedom that you have already spent. He treated the elimination of debt as a matter of dignity, not just math.

**Principle 4: Generosity creates increase.**
Proverbs 11:24-25 — *"One person gives freely, yet gains even more; another withholds unduly, but comes to poverty. A generous person will prosper."*

The hoarder's logic says: keep more, have more. Solomon's law says the opposite. The person who gives freely creates a flow that returns to them. The person who hoards disrupts the flow and loses.

**Principle 5: Desire without knowledge fails.**
Proverbs 19:2 — *"It is not good to have zeal without knowledge, nor to be hasty and miss the way."*

This is why lottery winners go broke. Why athletes lose fortunes. Why most businesses fail in year two. Desire — even enormous desire — cannot substitute for wisdom. Wealth without wisdom cannot hold.

**The principle:** Solomon's wealth was not the source of his success. It was the result of his wisdom. Get the order right.

## DAY 6

**Subject line:** Every relationship is either sharpening you or dulling you — there is no neutral
**Preview text:** Solomon had more to say about the people you choose than almost any other topic.

---

Yesterday was about money. Today is about something Solomon considered just as critical to the outcome of your life:

The people you allow closest to you.

Proverbs 27:17 — *"Iron sharpens iron, and one person sharpens another."*

When two pieces of iron rub against each other, one of two things happens. If the friction is right — if the other piece is harder, better-edged, purposeful — you come away sharper. More effective. Better than before.

But if the friction is wrong — if the other piece is dull, soft, or pulls in the wrong direction — you come away diminished.

The friction is the same. The result is entirely different depending on who is doing the sharpening.

Solomon identified several types of people to be very careful about:

**The talebearer.** Proverbs 16:28 — *"A perverse person stirs up conflict, and a gossip separates close friends."* The person who talks to you about others will talk to others about you. If you are their entertainment, you are not their friend.

**The angry person.** Proverbs 22:24 — *"Do not make friends with a hot-tempered person, do not associate with one easily angered, or you may learn their ways and get yourself ensnared."* Volatility is contagious. Prolonged exposure to someone's unmanaged anger will eventually shape how you manage yours.

**The flatterer.** Proverbs 28:23 — *"Whoever rebukes a person will in the end gain favor rather than one who has a flattering tongue."* The person who only tells you what you want to hear is not helping you. They are comfortable with your stagnation.

And what Solomon looked for in a true friend:

Proverbs 27:6 — *"Faithful are the wounds of a friend, but deceitful are the kisses of an enemy."*

A real friend tells you the truth — even when it costs them something. They see the better version of you even on your worst day, and they pull you toward it rather than accommodate your worst habits.

**The principle:** You are not just influenced by the people around you. You become like them. Solomon said to choose them the way you choose any major investment — carefully, intentionally, with a clear picture of what the return will be.

---

## DAY 7

**Subject line:** Where there is no vision, the people don't just stagnate — they perish
**Preview text:** Solomon's final word on what separates the people who build a life from the people life just happens to.

---

Six days ago you learned that Solomon asked for wisdom — and received everything else with it.

Today you get his single most clarifying statement about why most people never experience that kind of life:

*"Where there is no vision, the people perish."*
— Proverbs 29:18

The word perish here does not mean sudden catastrophe. It means to come undone gradually. To lose ground over time. To drift toward less.

And it starts the moment a person stops living by design and begins living by default.

Solomon drew a sharp line between these two ways of living.

The person **living by design** has a clear, specific, written picture of who they are becoming. They make daily decisions that move toward that picture. When hard things come — and they always come — that picture holds them on course. They are not building a life reactively. They are building it on purpose.

The person **living by default** wakes up one day and realizes that life happened to them instead of through them. The phone chose their attention. The expectations of others chose their priorities. The path of least resistance chose their direction. Their life is a collection of responses rather than a construction.

Default is not neutral. Something is always choosing your direction. The question is whether it is you.

Solomon described vision as a four-stage process:

**Sense it** — the "what if" stage. A thought, a possibility, a stirring that something more is available.

**See it** — the conceptualization stage. The picture gets specific. It becomes real in your mind before it is real in the world.

**Say it** — the manifestation stage. You begin to speak it — carefully at first, because premature sharing invites premature doubt — but you begin to give it language.

**Seize it** — the materialization stage. Daily, tangible steps. Physical action. This is where vision becomes a life.

And he connected vision directly to provision:
*"Where there is vision, there will always be provision."*

When a person has a clear vision and focuses their faith on it, the right people, resources, and opportunities begin to align. Solomon called this the Alignment Principle — not mysticism, but the observable result of a focused, faith-driven life.
